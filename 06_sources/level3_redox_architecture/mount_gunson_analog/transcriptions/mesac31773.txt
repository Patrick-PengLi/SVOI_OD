5. _ EXPLORATION ACTIVITIES

Pacminex activities in the area began with a number

of geological reconnaissance traverses towards the end of 1972,

before the exploration licence was applied for.

Geological mapping on 1:30,000 scale air photos has
been completed over the Birthday-Mt. Moseley-Oakden Hills area.
During mapping, rock chip samples were taken for geochemical
analysis. This work has indicated an area of anomalous copper
values in the vicinity of Little Selby Dam (see geochemical
plan).

A small power auger was used to take sub-surface soil
samples on a traverse south-west from Birthday Siding. The
depth of penetration was only 1 metre, which is considered
far too shallow to detect any geochemical anomalies which may
be present.

A scout drilling programme was completed in May and
June, 1973, using a Mayhew 1000 rotary drilling rig. Twelve
holes were drilled in the Oakden area and six in the MacFarlane
area. (Drill hole positions are marked on the 1:125,000 regional
geology map.) Table 2 gives a summary of the drilling results.

6. PANDURRA FORMATION

6.1 Distribution

Two major facies of the Pandurra Formation are
evident in the area. The main outcrops of Pandurra
Formation are along the ridge to the west of Pernatty
Lagoon, where the rock type is red quartzite, capped in
places by silcrete. Smaller outcrops occur in a series
from Selby Dam towards Old JRP Dam, with an isolated

occurrence to the east of Mt. Moseley. \

The second major facies occurs on the western
side of Lake MacFarlane, where the unit outcrops as
reddish-brown sandstone, partly capped by silcrete.

6.2 Quartzite Facies

Outcrops of this rock are the typical red
quartzites seen in the Pernatty Lagoon area - a very
tough rock, well bedded, with "liesegang" rings, cross
laminations, gritty horizons, and generally strong
fracturing.

_ In drill hole OK 12, 0.5 m of brecciated
quartzite was followed by 5 m of white, grey and pink
shale, then by 4m of interbedded quartzite and shale,
before massive quartzite was encountered. It may be
inferred from this that shale lenses are common through
the Pandurra Formation, but, being soft rocks, do not
form outcrops.

6.3 Sandstone Facies

The Lake MacFarlane area has not yet been’
mapped, so knowledge of the sandstone facies is somewhat
sketchy. In outcrops to the north of Six Mile Dam (on
the western side of the lake) the sandstone consists of
quartz, chert and acid volcanic grains, with scattered
pebbles of acid volcanics. Grains are sub-angular to
sub-rounded, and moderately well sorted. The rock is
much less compact than the quartzite facies.

The sandstone facies was intersected in drill
holes MF 1 and MF 2. MF 1 intersected 37 m of gritty
lithic sandstone, followed by 20 m of fine red sandstone.
The gritty sandstone consisted of rounded to sub-angular
grains, with noticeable authigenic overgrowths, made up
of about 75% quartz and 25% lithic grains (probably acid
volcanics). The red sandstone had a similar composition,

6.4 Geochemistry . .

Background geochemical values for Cu, Pb and
gn are quite low : 10-40 ppm Cu, < 20 ppm Pb and 5-40 ppm
Zn. No other elements have been determined. At the top
of the unit, somewhat higher values are associated with
obvious sulphide mineralisation in several drill holes,
notably OK 12 (220 ppm Cu).

Outcrops of Pandurra Quartzite south-west of
Old JRP Dam gave anomalous Cu geochemical values of up
to 120 ppm. To the west of Lake MacFarlane, outcrops of
red sandstone gave values up to 340 ppm Cu.

6.5 Post-Pandurra Surface

 

After lithification, the Pandurra Formation was
uplifted and exposed to the agencies of erosion, forming
a surface of gentle ridges and broad valleys. This erosional
surface is marked by a quartzite breccia with a red clay
and sand matrix, similar to that found in the Cattle Grid
area. This probably represents a fossil scree slope or

soil profile.

Drilling and mapping show that the main Pandurra
ridge on the western side of Pernatty Lagoon extends into
the Oakden Hills area as a complex of low ridges and valleys
(see Structural and Palaeogeographic Map) . To the west and
south of Oakden Hills, the post-Pandurra surface slopes
away to a broad shallow basin of deposition, rising again
at Lake MacFarlane in the west, and in the vicinity of
Strawbridge Hill to the south. The depth to the top of
Pandurra Formation in the basin is as shown in Table 3.

7. WOOCALLA DOLOMITE

7.1 Distribution -

The Woocalla Dolomite is not continuous over
the whole of the area. It fills depressions in the post-
Pandurra surface, and forms an on-lap structure onto the
Pandurra ridges. Away from the ridges, the dolomite
undergoes a facies change to a deeper water black shale

  

 

facies.

There are no outcrops of the unit in the area,
put it was intersected in a number of drill holes. In
the northern part of the area, in the vicinity of Old
Oakden Hills homestead, the dolomite laps onto the main
] : Pandurra ridge, and was intersected in drill hole OK 11.

Holes OK 2 to OK 6 were drilled on an area of Pandurra
"highs", and no dolomite was intersected, although chips
of dolomite at the base of the Whyalla Sandstone in OK 4
indicate the proximity of dolomite in this locality.

7.2 Dolomite Facies

The term "dolomite facies" actually encompasses a
variety of dolomitic rock types as shown by mapping in the
Pernatty Lagoon and Woocalla areas. However, in the
Qakden Hills-Lake MacFarlane area, lack of outcrop prevents
the mapping of facies changes in the dolomite. :

A number of variations are evident in the drill
cuttings. OK 11 intersected 3.5 m of tough white to pale
brown dolomite overlain by 6 m of dark grey dolomitic mud.
OK 10 intersected 2 m of tough white to pale grey dolomite
overlain by 10.5 m of dark grey dolomitic shale. MF 1 was
drilled through 8 m of finely laminated grey to brown shaley
dolomite.
