THE ADELAIDE GEOSYNCLINE AND STUART SHELF
INTRODUCTION

An arcuate belt of Precambrian sediments which overlie unconformably
older crystalline basement extends northerly and westerly across South Australia.
The sediments represent the cover of a former more extensive platform. The most
complete record of. these and overlying Cambrian sequences is preserved in the
Adelaide Geosyncline (Sprigg, 1952). The Geosyncline is an important metallo-
genic province and in this paper is described as extending from the Kangaroo
Island - Mount Lofty Ranges area near Adelaide te the Willouran Ranges near
Marree in the north.

The time term Adelaidean is applied to the Precambrian sequences pre-
served in the Geesyncline. The following table outlines the scheme of
Adelaidean time subdivisions and rock groupings adopted by the Geological Sur-

vey to describe these sequences.

The largely undeformed and relatively thin Adelaidean remnant cover
on the Gawler Craton to the west of the Geosyncline defined the Stuart Shelf.
The complex transition zone between Shelf and Geosyncline is the Torrens Hinge
Zone, a near-meridional feature that forms part of a continental fracture
pattern of major tectonic and metallogenic significance.

For details of stratigraphic sequences readers are referred to the
articles listed in the short bibliography, and in particular, the Handbook of
South Australian Geology (L.W. Parkin, Ed. 1969). Below are a few comments on
each of the main rock groups.

In the Mount Painter region the Callanna Beds rest unconformably on
crystalline basement about 1 600 m.y. old (Carpentarian): they form a thick
sequence both here and in the Willouran Ranges. The lower Callanna Beds are
separated from the upper Callanna Beds by an erosional break and contain the
Wooltana Volcanics which are possibly to be correlated with unnamed volcanics
in the Peake and Denison Ranges, the Wilangee Volcanics in western New South
Wales (Rose and Brunker, 1971, p. 111) and blocks in diapirs. The position of
the River Wakefield "Group" in relation to the upper Callanna Beds (with which
it was previously correlated) is now in doubt, since recent work by W.V. Preiss
(personal communication, 1973) suggests that it lies above the Rhynie Sandstone
in the Burra Group.

The Burra Group seems to represent continuation of the shallow water
conditions in which the Callanna Beds were deposited and contains in the
Skillogalee Dolomite, sedimentary magnesite beds, stromatolites (Preiss, 1971)

and fossil algal filaments (Schopf and Barghoorn, 1969).

The Umberatana Group is characterised by tillitic rocks represent-
ing three phases of glaciation. The Braemar Iron Formation, possibly of
lacustrine origin, lies between the two earliest phases. The first and
second phases of glaciation were preceeded by tectonism.

The Wilpena Group features thick sequences of silty and finely sandy
rocks and contains near its top the Ediacara Fauna. The overlying Cambrian has
been described by Daily (1972).

The Lower Cambrian is rich in carbonate rocks (Daily, 1969) and
records a marine transgression. In the Flinders Ranges the Lower Cambrian
Hawker Group was succeeded after a slight hiatus (disconformable base of the
Billy Creek Formation) by red beds of the Middle to ?Upper Cambrian Lake Frome
Group.

TECTONIC EVOLUTION OF THE ADELAIDE GEOSYNCLINE
Tectonic Setting

The Adelaide Geosyncline is flanked on the west and east by the Gawler
Craton and Willyama and Mount Painter Inliers comprising Precambrian crystalline
basement complexes radiometrically dated as older than 1 400.m.y. The term Geo-
syncline is applied because of the great thickness, in excess of 24 000 metres
(80 000 ft.) represented by the Adelaidean and Cambrian sediments within this
framework. The Geosyncline marks an ancient region of platform downwarp bounded
by marginal rifts. The basement underwent intervals of tectonism followed by
quiescent periods of gentle subsidence. The sequences were deformed in the
Adelaidean and in the Cambro-Ordovician. The sediments preserved are generally
of continental-lagoonal-shallow marine facies mainly derived from the crystal-
line basement in the west. The Geosyncline was intermittently connected to

other basins to the north and east.

Early Willouran Environments and Moonta - Wallaroo Mineralisation and
Middle Willouran Tectonism

Flood basalts, of the Wooltana Volcanics type with minor copper
mineralisation, were a feature of the early Adelaidean. They probably reflect
tensional rifting and are associated with carbonate sedimentation and evapo-
rities. The dating of the earliest Adelaidean is dependent on the correlation
between the Wooltana and the Roopena Volcanics (1 345 m.y.) on the eastern and
western flanks of the Geosyncline. Volcanics and metasiltstones in drill] holes
in the Willamulka - Bute area east of Moonta - Wallaroo district are either
correlated with the Roopena Volcanics or an older sequence. These rocks were
deformed and metasomatised, presumably by the 1 200 m.y. event* recorded radio-
metrically by A.W. Webb (pers. comm.) in granites in the Moonta region. This
event probably records the emplacement of the Moonta - Wallaroo copper lodes.
The carbonate metasomatism, early deformation and possible igneous intrusion of
the lower Callanna Beds in the Geosyncline may have been initiated at about the
time of the Moonta mineralisation event in structures which were to evolve into
centres of diapirism. This activity may reflect basement movements associated

with the Musgravian Orogeny.

Late Willouran and Torrensian Environments

 

The thick sequences of the continental and shallow marine upper
Callanna Beds, River Wakefield Group and Burra Group and an unknown proportion of
the Duff Creek Formation to the north and the Pandurra Formation to the west

followed the earlier Willouran tectonism.

The Sturtian - Marinoan Interval

 

Uplift, diapirism and graben development in the north marked the
onset of the earliest Sturtian glaciation. The subsequent interglacial
interval was probably associated with an eustatic rise in sea level and cli-
matic change. The Sturtian disconformity is a locus for later copper mineral-
isation at the base of the Woecalla Dolomite, as is the contact between Sturtian
glacials and pyritic Tindelpina Shale Member in the Geosyncline. Following the
Marinoan ‘glaciation, there was again transgression onto the Stuart Shelf. This
Marinoan transgression is a lecus for the Mount Gunson stratiform copper mineral-

isation in the Whyalla Sandstone Member.

Cambrian Sedimentation, Tectonism and the Delamerian Orogeny

The shallow water Marinoan sedimentation of largely western provenance,
was terminated by uplift over much of the Geosyncline. The onset of the Cam-
brian was marked by a change to an eastern provenance and a widespread change to
a marine shelf carbonate environment. Early Cambrian volcanism occurred in the
south. Geochemical evidence (Thomson 1965) indicates that lead, copper and zinc
enrichments were widespread at the transition zone and associated with Mississippi
Valley type ore occurrences (e.g. Edjacara lead). Late Lower Cambrian positive
movements followed. Following a brief return to marine carbonate deposition in
the Middle Cambrian, shallow water and continental sedimentation terminating pro-
bably in the Upper Cambrian, marked the onset of the Delamerian Orogeny (Thomson,
1969). The orogeny transformed the Adelaide Geosyncline into a fold belt and
concluded with the intrusion of Ordovician granite (about 450 m.y.) in the east
and south, e.g. at Anabama and Bendigo (S.A.) associated with low grade copper
mineralisation.

The regional metamorphic gradient across the Geosyncline led to an
extensive reheating of the Geosyncline basement which was probably already

mineralised and thus provided a secondary source of metal by fluid transfer.

Diapirism, carbonate intrusion and metasomatism was. particularly active at
basement structural intersections which were also the loci for basic and pro-
bably acid intrusion, e.g. porphyry at Burra. It is likely that the majority
of the base metal deposits in the Geosyncline were finally localised in their
particular stratigraphic or structural settings as a result of the Delamerian
Orogeny. Large scale movements of intrastratal fluids containing traces of
base metals commenced during diagenesis and with increasing pressure and tem-
perature moved upwards around diapirs and westward to the Torrens Hinge Zone
where wedge-outs and disconformities between favourable facies became the sites

for stratiform base metal mineralisation.
