i. INTRODUCTION

The McLeay Beds constitute a stratigraphic transition
— zone between Pandurra Formation and overlying Whyalla Sandstone
at McLeay railway sidings 21 km south-west of Mt. Gunson.

Six specimens were submitted for examination.

2. SUMMARY

A9501 (71450)

A relatively small number of large spherical quartz
grains occur, but larger more angular grains of polymict
origin are common. A two population distribution of quartz
rock particles with more rounded silt-size particles are

baal seen. Resemblance to sample A9967 (80126) is not good but
there is a better resemblance to specimen P.39 from the

nm Pandurra District (Ref. 1), though to be a ferruginous

Whyalla Sandstone. However, the larger more angular grains

and the low abundance of heavy minerals and ferruginous

cherts place the rock outside standard Whyalla Sandstone

classification.

a9502 (71451)

The rock has a distinctly upper (?) Paridurra Quartzite
appearance and is somewhat similar to specimens selected from
West Lagoon, Woocalla Quarry, etc.

A9503 (71452)

The rock consists of mainly subangular even-grained
quartz and contains appreciable ferruginous cherts and heavy
minerals. It does not completely fit in with either Whyalla
Sandstone or Pandurra Quartzite types but has some properties
of each of the stratigraphic units.

In thin-section the rock is seen to be a silicified
quartzite of mainly subangular or rounded subangular grains
varying between 0.2 and 1.5 mm with a median grain size of
0.4 mm. Many grains were made subangular by silicification
and originally more regular grains are present as rounded or
oblong nuclei demarcated by hematite particle and fluid
inclusion trains from authigenic overgrowths.

One large quartz rock fragment erratic, several milli-
metres in length and width, contains quartz grains of similar
size and shape to the host rock but the cement between the
grains is chalcedonic silica and hematite/limonite, the latter
being more abundant than is seen in the host rock interstices.

Groups of heavy minerals, topaz, sphene and epidote also occur.

Another fragment of red quartzite present shows
abundant chert and hematite between larger quartz grains arid

also the presence of subangular silt-size quartz matrix.

One other fragment of red quartzite shows the presence
of heavy minerals of magnetite with less zircon and sphene
which are partly obscured by limonite/hematite matrix as seen
in a red quartzite fragment in A9966, but the matrix in A9966
is illite/kaolinite where not silicified with quartz. The
overall textures, grain size and mineral composition of A9966
(Pandurra Quartzite ~ Ref. 1) are in general very similar to
A9502 as seen in thin-section and both specimens contain the
quite large erratics.

The size and shapes of quartz grains and distribution
of erratics are very similar to: Pandurra Quartzite specimens
from A4009 (West Lagoon), A4226, A4230 (Woocalla Quarry),
A4250 (Pernatty Lagoon) and A4254 (Main Open Cut), (Ref. 4).
However, A4250 has a cherty cement and A4254 retains abundant
kaolinite in the interstices. Also the very large (several
mm's) clasts of quartz rock are not apparent in thin-section
in any of these specimens.

A9503 (71452) (Figure 4)

Bedded quartzite, finer grained than a9502. Fine
sugary pink quartzite specked with opaques in places.
Surface of the rock is pitted.

The rock is seen to consist of even-grained mainly
subangular quartz grains. Many quartz grains (range 0.03
to 0.4 mm, median 0.2 mm) show overgrowths, the rock being
completely silicified; occasional large erratics of quartz
of approximately 1 mm size occur as quite rounded grains.
Up to half the quartz grains were originally quite round or
oval as indicated by internal narrow trains of hematite
particles and fluid inclusions. Silicification of rounded
silt-size quartz grains is seen locally. Minimal inter-
stitial chert occurs in pockets and channels. Rounded
ferruginous cherts and sericitic ferruginous cherts (range
in size,0.1 to 0.8 mm), as are normally common in Whyalla
Sandstone are present to 3 volume percent.

Scattered, rounded zircons (N 0.10 mm) “occur to less
than 1 volume percent of rock and less rounded green tourmaline
and sphene of similar or slightly larger grain size are seen.
Rounded vugs (“ 0.3 mm) are common to 3 volume percent of the
rock. The abundance of originally rounded quartz, ferruginous
cherts and appreciable although not abundant heavy minerals
are not typical characters of Pandurra Quartzite but are
characteristic of Whyalla Sandstone. The evenness of grains
with sporadic quartz erratics is typical of some Pandurra
Quartzite fabrics.

A9504_ (71453)

'Green' quartzite - dark coloured laminated rock

with cavities.

Mainly subangular, medium size quartz in a dark limo-
mite and chert matrix. Large rounded and subangular quartz
erratics (”1 mm) occur in a fairly even-grained mass of quartz
grains (0.1 to 0.6 mm, median 0.4 mm). Silicification by chert
has taken place and this gives the rock the green colour. Also
broad bands of limonite particles occur just within the
periphery of many quartz grains and particularly where they
adjoin the chert matrix. The chert matrix constitutes about
10 volume percent of the rock and forms broad parallel laminae
up to 1 mm or more in width containing islands of quartz grains
Some of this chert has a dark limonite stain. Occasional,

(<1 volume percent of rock) clear detrital cherts are seen.
These tend to be rounded and are of similar grain size to the
neighbouring quartz grains.

Some of the clear and ferruginous (non-volcanic)
chert of the Whyalla Sandstone may have originated from this
horizon. There are definite similarities between interstitial
chert seen here to many of the chert particles observed
commonly in the Whyalla Sandstone. The rock has been weathered
to the extent of Pandurra Quartzite in the Main Open Cut
Gunson which also has abundant cherty matrix.

Grit-quartz pebbles and coarse sand. An irregular

Not unlike Pandurra Quartzite on a coarser scale.
Poorly sorted quartz grains (range 0.2 to 8.0 mm). Many of
the larger grains are quite angular and are quartz rock
fragments. The smaller grains (mainly quartz mineral), show
rounded grain nuclei with more angular overgrowths, the
original grains being outlined by fine particle hematite
inclusions trains. Occasional large quartz gneiss grains

are commonly relatively small (“0.6 mm) and rounded.
Ferruginous cherts are of similar size and shape to clear
cherts and occur to about 1 volume percent of the rock.
Occasional cavities occur between larger quartz grains which
may have contained clay mineral which was lost in section
preparation,otherwise commonly the rock is a silicified quartz
Mosaic. Rare, rounded zircon grains (0.03 mm) are seen
embedded in quartz overgrowths. No quartz silt-size material
eccurs and no ferruginous cherts with volcanic textures are

seen. Heavy minerals are rare.
A silicified gravel.

In thin-section some large quartz rock fragments
are seen to be not so rounded and are cherty looking. How-
ever, most large grains are rounded but are not spherical;
two fragments are distinctly triangular in shape. The source
of the large grains is variable, some being gnéissose :
metamorphic. Others are coarse quartzite fragments with
intricately sutured quartz grains; yet others consist of
very fine even-grain quartzite as seen in some clasts of
Pandurra Quartzite and in the fabric of A9503 of this series.
The rock matrix consists of typical even-grained Pandurra
Quartzite where this occurs, of quartz grains 0.2 to 1.0 mm
but mainly close to median size 0.3 mm. Many of these grains
were guite rounded but quartz overgrowths have made the grains
somewhat. subangular and have produced a silicified mosaic but
with no intricate suturing between quartz grains as is seen
in some of the large clasts. Small rounded ferruginous
cherts (“0.3 mm) which occur to about 1 volume percent of
the rock are rounded but no volcanic textures are seen. One
large subangular ferruginous chert (10 mm in width) occurs.
Apart from hematite (after magnetite?) grains in one of the
large cherty clasts and occasional specks of opaque mineral
in small ferruginous cherts there are no heavy minerals to be
seen in this rock.

: In A9862 (70743), Whyalla Sandstone described as a
ferruginous deposit from Elizabeth Creek, the large clasts
are several millimetres in diameter but are quite spherical
in shape and are smaller than the clasts seen in A9506. How-
ever, there is a silt-size quartz where seen through the
dense limonite cement but again these quartz grains are
smaller than those in the matrix of A9506.

There cre few similarities between the Elizabeth
Creek, Whyalla Sandstone A9862 and the rock A9506 presently
discussed. A common origin of some grains (cherts, gneiss

fragments, etc.) is suspected.
