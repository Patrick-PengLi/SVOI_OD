‘ INTRODUCTION

In December, 1969, it was decided that the
mining at Mount Gunson, scheduled to start in late
April, 1970, would commence with the development
of the East Lagoon Orebody.

The East Lagoon Orebody was selected because
it appeared to contain the highest grade sulfide
mineralisation even though the potential tonnage of
ore was not as great as other deposits in the area.
Equally important in the decision was the fact that
drilling had suggested greater continuity of mineral-
isation in the East Lagoon deposit than in any of the
other mineralised zones.

PREVIOUS DRILLING AND ESTIMATED RESERVES

 

The first drilling in the East Lagoon area
was carried out in late 1967. Significant copper
values were encountered in 12 percussion drill holes
located on 200' x 100' centers. Eight of these
percussion holes indicated a moderate size zone of
potential ore.

In March, 1968, eight diamond drill holes
were located immediately adjacent to the eight
percussion holes. Only a fair degree of correlation
was found to exist between some of the assays in the
diamond drill holes and the adjacent percussion holes.

OBJECTIVES OF NEW DRILLING PROGRAMME

In order to prepare the East Lagoon Orebody
for mining it was necessary to obtain the following
pertinent information :-

l. The degree of continuity of mineralisation
between the holes drilled in previous
drilling programmes.

 

2. The areas and lateral limits of economic .
mineralisation.

 

3. More accurate ore reserve estimates.

 

4. Depths to lithologic units that would
effect mining techniques.

To obtain this information a detailed geophysical
survey and closely spaced drilling were scheduled to be
carried out over the East Lagoon deposit.

DRILLING

PROGRAMME

The drilling programme consisted of four

continuous phases :-

1.

2.

Fill-in drilling between drill holes until
the area of known mineralisation was covered
by a 100' x 100! grid.

Drilling outward along each drill section at
50' centers until the lateral limits of
mineralisation were determined.

Drilling at least two waste holes on each
drill section beyond the limits of mineral-
isation.

Testing areas around the four holes encount-
ering mineralisation outside the main mineral-
ised zone and delineating if potential economic
mineralisation was indicated.

SAMPLING AND LOGGING

During the drilling of each hole the zones of
potential ‘mineralisation were sampled on two foot
intervals. The samples were simultaneously logged
to obtain data on depths to lithologic units,
percentage and species of minerals encountered and
estimated grades of copper.

The samples were split to convenient size;
wet samples being dried before splitting. The
samples were subsequently bagged in numbered envelopes
for shipment to the assay laboratory.

ASSAYING

The samples were sent to the C.S.R. Central
Laboratory for preparation and copper analysis by
atomic absorption. Results from the laboratory were
reported as percent total copper.

Each drill hole showing significant copper
values was subsequently composited. The composited
samples were analysed by atomic absorption for silver.
Results were reported in parts per million which were
converted to ounces (reoy) per short ton.

Five composited drill holes have been selected
for complete analysis. These samples will be analysed
as soon as the new x-ray system at the Central
Laboratory is operational.

DRILLING PROBLEMS AND SAMPLE RELIABILITY

Throughout the drilling programme various sampling
problems have been apparent.

As long as the rock being drilled is dry the
drilling is rapid and there is good sample reliability.
If the hole is drilled quickly it can often be completed
before significant water enters the hole. As soon as
the samples become wet, however, sample reliability drops
and drilling is impeded. The drilling of a new hole and
casing through the water producing zone is often necessary
to achieve even fair sample reliability.

The most troublesome water producing zone in
the East Lagoon is in the lower part of the lithic
sandstone immediately on top of the disconformity with
the red quartzite. Sample reliability in this zone
was often poor and it is probable that the better copper
values expected at this interface were often inadequately
sampled.

The percussion drilling has occasionally led to
the salting of copper values. After the drilling of a
wet hole some ‘copper values may remain in the sampling
system and contaminate the samples from the next hole.
Adequate cleaning of the sampling system is necessary to
avoid this type of salting. Salting may also occur
after the drill has penetrated a mineralised section with
copper values from above contaminating samples from below.

Recognition of salting was not always possible.
In all cases where salting was detected the erroneous
assays have been disregarded.

Upon occasion the sampling of a hole did not begin
soon enough and sometimes drilling or sampling was stopped

SUMMARY OF DRILLING RESULTS

GENERAL

The drilling programme was scheduled to commence
in early January, 1970. However, due to a lack of
equipment, actual production drilling did not start
until early February. Drilling progress was slow
with poor sample reliability and it was not until an
experienced driller had been obtained that the drilling
progressed smoothly and rapidly.

In early March the drilling was terminated
before the objectives of the programme could be achieved.
This was due to the departure of the experienced driller
who had previous commitments elsewhere.

The final drill hole co-ordinates and assay
results were completed by late March.

A total of 60 locations were sampled. At many
locations, however, more than one hole was necessary
before adequate sampling could be achieved. In some
cases the data from two or more holes were combined so
as to form an idealized hole considered as representative
of the area. The data is thus presented as 60 drill
holes.

Of the 60 drill holes 40 intersected significant
copper mineralisation and 20 were drilled in waste.

As mentioned above, the drilling was terminated
before the objectives of the programme were fully
realized. Fill-in drilling to a 100' x 100' grid was
completed. Internal continuity of ore grade and thick-
ness for the center of the main zone of mineralisation
was substantiated.

The lateral limits of the mineralisation have not
been determined. Step-out drilling along drill sections
at 50' centers continued to encounter significant copper
mineralisation.

Mineralisation trends on the west, east, and
northeast sides are still open and considerable reserves
may yet be found in these directions.

ORE RESERVE ESTIMATES

The ore reserves have been divided into three
categories based upon the degree of assurance of
existance. The classes are as follows :-

Measured Ore Ore delineated by detailed drilling
so that size, shape, and mineral
content are well established.

Indicated Ore Ore lacking in sufficient drilling so
that size, shape, and mineral content
are uncertain.

Inferred Ore No drilling; estimates based upon
assumed continuity for which there is
geologic evidence.

ASSUMPTIONS
1. Sulphide cut-off grade = 0.4% total copper.
2. Specific gravity of ore = 2.60,

3. Mining can be effected from one level, hence
: no significant lateral dilution of ore.

4. Mining control will admit no more than one foot
above and one foot below mineralisation as
dilution material.

METHOD

The accompanying Plan of Ore Reserve Blocks
shows the areas assigned to each ore category.

Blocks 1-55 were calculated by the triangular
method. The peripheral blocks 56-83 were calculated
by the rectangular method. The two results were
combined to give the measured ore reserves.

The indicated ore, blocks 84-90 and the inferred
ore, blocks 91-93 were calculated by the rectangular
method.

Blocks 94 and 95 are isolated from the main zone
of mineralisation and each contain one percussion drill
hole that encountered significant copper mineralisation.
Both areas require drilling to assess their potential.

SILVER

As previously mentioned, each drill hole with
