2.3 Pandurra Formation Weathered Zone

The ore zone of the Cattle Grid is principally
the brecciated zone created during the weathering of the
Pandurra Formation. An unmineralised example of this has
been exposed near the new tailings dam. The sequence
from surface to 20 cm, consists of clay, floaters of
quartzite and silcrete and a poorly developed calcium
carbonate nodular zone. This is followed by a zone, about
1.5 - 2.0 m thick, of quartzite showing the remnants of

3. MINERALOGY AND ORE HABIT

 

Macroscopic features observed since the development
of the ore-face include :-

3.1 Pyrite nodules within Whyalla Sandstone. -These
are of two types either developed along thin beds of coarse _
sandstone or else developed immediately above the Pandurra
contact. They comprise massive pyrite cementing grains of
Whyalla Sandstone with proportions of copper sulphides
varying from 20 ppm to 20%, High grade samples containing
chalcopyrite and chalcocite are located at the top of the
ore zone, :

3.2 Proceeding downwards in the section from the top
of the ore zone pyrite-chalcopyrite and usually the first °
minerals encountered. They are commonly associated with

a clay rich breccia.

3.3 When the top of the quartzite is slump folded up
into the overlying sandstone, or where the surface has well
developed rolls the trails of chalcocite enrich the Whyalla
Sandstone occasionally up to ore grade values.

3.4 The upper brecciated quartzite zone of the Pandurra
Formation is the richest and carries the most consistent
mineralisation within the ore sections. Minerals are found
occupying spaces between the fragmented quartzite which

still retain evidence of bedding:in most places. Minor:
folding is common with the centre portions of the folds
often containing an enrichment in mineralisation. In the
present pit (October, 1974), chalcocite and bornite are the
predominant minerals with a significant amount of sphalerite,
minor galena and traces of chalcopyrite.

A thin grey clay seam is usually located partway

through this brecciated zone and is considered to be part
of the original rock unit.

A. MINERAL ZONING

 

Vertical mineral zoning appears to be very haphazard
but in general there is a pyrite/chalcopyrite rich band at the
top of the ore zone grading to a chalcocite/bornite assemblage
to the lower sections of the mineralised body. Sphalerite and
galena occur usually in the upper portion of the ore section,

Lateral zoning is not apparent in mineragraphic
studies, “however, metallurgical test results do suggest a
zonation of some metals and thus by association the various
mineral types.

From the plan of metallurgical concentrate values
the following association can be inferred :-

(a) a western zone of high grade copper, arsenic

and iron. . tp sane
(b) an eastern zone of high grade copper, bismuth
and lead.

(c) a transitional zone between the two zones,
where low copper, high bismuth, high arsenic,
high zinc and high cobalt occur.

The iron, arsenic and cobalt values are considered
to be from the primary sulphide minerals deposited - pyrite,
carrollite, arsenopyrite and gersdorffite.

The high copper values suggest some contined enrich-
ment from the source of the metal ions.

Taking into account the mineralisation located at
Mc 14 and the general NE trend of the minerals in the Cattle
Grid, it would appear likely that the source of metallisation
came from the immediate NE corner, that is the basic dyke
occurring near MG 14.

5. THE PANDURRA SURFACE AND MINERAL DEPOSITION

From the contour map of the old Pandurra surface at
the Cattle Grid it can be seen that the trend of mineralisation
cuts across a basinal structure and over a low ridge. There is
no evidence of a 'high water' mark. This feature is also
noticeable in Gunyah Lake and the MOC-House Workings.

By observing the structure contour map of the general
Mount Gunson area it can be seen that the MG 14 area sits in a
saddle of a ridge separating the Cattle Grid from the gully and
its adjacent deposits, as well as the Gunyah Lake deposits. This
also suggests that the basic dyke may have been the source of
metal ions,

It could be inferred that the metal rich water washing:
down from the saddle precipitated in the dolomite shales of the
guily, and along the edges of the dolomite in favourable locations.
Referring back to work carried out on the MOC-House in 1971-72,

a strong NW trend to the mineralisation had been produced by
contouring grade and thickness. This trend is sub-parallel to
the trend of the gully. There is also a matching trend in
mineralisation extending from the Gunyah Lake to south of the
copper concentrator, On the western side, the Cattle Grid has
@ pronounced NE-SW trend pointing towards the MG 14 area.

The basic dyke intersected by LY3 in’ the MG 14 area
was discovered after testing a ground magnetometer anomaly.
The dyke consists of basaltic material with a possible acid
differentiate on its lower contact. It is extremely weathered
in the uppermost portions, to the point of being a green to
buff coloured clay. The clay material does not penetrate into
the overlying Woocalla Dolomite shales and the dyke is believed
to be older than the Woocalla Dolomite. It is possible that
magma responsible for the dyke actually extruded onto the
surface as lava. Evidence for this may exist in the hematitic
clays intersected in the Cattle Grid diamond drilling, and the
red clay channelways found in West Lagoon during previous mining
operations.

7. DISCUSSION

It has been established that the basic dyke at MG 14
contains high background metal values. All the copper deposits
known at Mount Gunson are located directly within the downstream
influence of the basic dyke and possible extruded associates.

It could be argued that the subsequent emplacement of the metal
ions into the ore-body concentrations occurred by groundwater
circulation. However, although groundwater circulation-is
considered to be responsible for the concentration of metal ions
in the Whyalla Sandstone, a prior mechanism is believed to be
responsible for the formation of the ore-bodies in the Pandurra
Formation. In the attached Figure 1 a sampling programme across
the Pandurra contact shows extremely high concentration of metal
values existing within the Pandurra surface breccia, whilst an
envelope of low values extends into the Whyalla Sandstone, :
directly relateable to the form of the Pandurra surface, There
is no physical difference between the open porous Whyalla sSand-
stone and the Pandurra surface, yet the economic mineralisation
is strongly localised in the Pandurra surface breccia. A -
logical conclusion to this is that the mineralisation is older
than the Whyalla Sandstone. Further proof of this age relation—
ship are the red clay channelways found in the Cattle Grid Pit.
These can be interpreted as stream channels developed and
infilled at the beginning of Whyalla time. Red clay and
quartzite rubble form the base of these streams followed by
Whyalla clay and sandstone with well rounded pebbles of quartzite.
The Pandurra surface and associated mineralisation has been
washed away, leaving low grade to barren portions which often
contain high grade mineralisation on either side.

theory :-

containing the greatest concentration of primary
minerals - arsenic, iron, copper and cobalt.

8. CONCLUSION

Opening up of the Cattle Grid Pit has greatly eluci-
dated the geology of the Whyalla Sandstone and the ore~bearing
zone of the Pandurra Formation. With this fresh knowledge and
the discovery of the MG 14 basic dyke and overlying mineralisation,
a re-appraisal of the ore-genesis theories proposed for Mount
Gunson has taken place. The final picture is still unclear, but
it is considered that the possible importance of this basic
igneous activity directly related to copper mineralisation at
Mount Gunson, must be taken into account in future regional
exploration.
