i. INTRODUCTION ©

0590740

Specimens from the Gully and MG 14 areas, drill holes
were sent to Head Office for petrographic examination of rock
types and mineragraphic study to determine abundance of copper
sulphides, grain sizes of the sulphides and complexity of
composite sulphides. In particular, the abundance of sphalerite
and carrollite and their attachments with copper sulphides were
observed. The intergrowths of copper sulphides with syngenetic
pyrite were of interest. In both prospecting areas the abundance

of clay minerals was of particular interest.

. Drill Hole ~ Gully 1

23.15 m_ (A 9716)

The specimen is mainly fine carbonate with some illite and
detrital quartz as bands or erratics. Some quartz is
sharply angular. Sulphide present is pyrite and is mainly
framboidal. Possible detrital (?) pyrite occurs in
@etrital quartz bands. :

27.30 m (A 9717)
Graded beds of subangular to sharply angular fine quartz

possibly derived from an adjoining tillite deposit with
very little if any reworking. Illite and kaolinite occur,
variably abundant at adjoining horizons.

No banded concentration of sulphides occur but they are
present scattered throughout, mainly as framboids and
individual tiny spheres of pyrite. Traces of chalcocite
are seen replacing pyrite. Occasional subangular coarse
pyrite grains present. may be detrital but sulphide is
largely syngenetic.

27.80 m_ (A 9718)

No graded bedding is seen, but coarser silty quartz bands
contain a high concentration of pyrite framboids (0.015 mm)
which show internal replacement by bornite>digenite or
chalcocite. The chalcocite where it occurs has extended
itself by replacement over several framboids to 0.04 mm

to 0.08 mm and is coarse enough to be recoverable but there
is not much of it, only occasional bands up to 0.3 mm
which are largely unreplaced pyrite.

Pyrite present is syngenetic and the copper sulphides are
epigenetic (diagenetic). Other gangue minerals. present
are illite and kaolinite occurring variably with the quartz
in 'bands'.

20.3 m (A 9719)
Lenses of dark dolomite lie in clear dolomite matrix.
