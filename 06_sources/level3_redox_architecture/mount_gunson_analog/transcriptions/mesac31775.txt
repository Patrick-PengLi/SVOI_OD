1. SUMMARY

This report covers work carried out on E.L. 29 from
8th June to 19th July, 1973, and subsequently on E.L. 81, from
20th July to 19th October, 1973. E.L. 81 is an amalgamation
incorporating E.L. 29.

During this period exploration activity has consisted
of :-:

1.1 Drilling
42 holes were drilled totalling 2,190 metres
including 171 metres of diamond drilling.

1.2 Geological Mapping

This was carried out in the Mt. Moseley and
Lake Windabout areas (refer Appendix I).
1.3 Geochemistry
. _Further evaluation of samples from drill holes +
over the Cattle Grid deposit delineated an anomaly at
shallow depths over the deposit. On the basis of this |
orientation work on a grid pattern of shallow drill {
samples may show up targets of possible mineralisation l
at depth. Grid lines are now being laid out in

- preparation for such a shallow geochemical drilling

programme.

1.4 Geophysical Surveys

Orientation surveys over the undisturbed
Cattle Grid copper deposit have been undertaken (refer
Appendix II).

On 20th July, 1973, E.L. 29 was amalgamated
with adjoining licence areas applied for by Mount Gunson
Mines, to become part of E.L. 81.

This report covers work carried out on E.L. 29
from 8th June to 19th July, 1973, and subsequent work
carried out on E.L. 81, for the first quarterly period
ending 19th October, 1973.

3.3 Geophysics

Orientation geophysical surveys were carried
out during the period 18th to 3lst July, 1973, and again
from the 31st August to 14th September, 1973. These
surveys were carried out over the Cattle Grid deposit
to test the applicability of various geophysical techniques
on an established orebody before disturbance by open cast

mining.

3.3.1 Geophysical Methods

(i) Ground Magnetics.
(ia) Self Potential.
(iii) Induced Polarisation including pulse pole-

dipole, dipole-dipole, a saturation test down
‘two drill holes and Mise-a-la-masse down one .
_ drill hole.

. (iv) INPUT (airborne E.M.).
(v) Hammer Seismic.

(vi) 2 Coil Electro-Magnetics.

The results of surveys are still to be
interpreted in detail and final reports await this
work. A separate summary (by the Chief Geophysicist,
Pacminex) is appended (Appendix II).

3.3.2 Preliminary Results

(i) Ground Magnetics : some small anomaly effects
may be due to mineralisation but a general low

is not apparent.

1. SUMMARY

Mapping and drilling in the Birthday-Mmt. Moseley-
Oakden Hills area has revealed that the main ridge of Pandurra
Formation (stretching along the western side of Pernatty Lagoon)
extends into the Oakden Hills area as a complex of buried ridges
separated by shallow valleys. To the north, west and south of
the ridge complex, the post-Pandurra surface slopes away into
broad basins of deposition, infilled with the black shale facies
of the Woocalla Dolomite.

A scout drilling programme between Oakden Hills and
Lake MacFarlane has shown that the black shale basin extends
westward to Lake MacFarlane. On the western side cf the lake,
the post-Pandurra surface rises to another Pandurra ridge.

Target areas for further exploration are the slopes
of the Pandurra ridges where the Woocalla Dolomite forms an
on~-lap structure onto the Pandurra Formation. in particular,
on the basis of high copper values, three areas have been
delineated for follow-up work.

2. PHYSIOGRAPHY

« The area consists mainly of a sandy plain with
generally sparse vegetation and scattered low rock outcrops.
Across the northern part of the area, stretching from the
Transcontinental Railway Line to Island Laggon, the sandy
plain grades into sand dune country with quite a thick
covering of stunted trees and almost no outcrops. Salt lakes
occupy a large portion of the area. In the east, the land
rises slightly to the ridge of Pandurra Quartzite which extends
along the western shore of Pernatty Lagoon. Similarly, in the
west the land rises to another ridge of Pandurra Formation on
the western shore of Lake MacFarlane. Prominent outcrops of
Whyalla Sandstone occur along the western edge of Island Lagoon
and Lake Finniss. In the centre of the area, four mesas, capped
by Simmens Quartzite, form prominent features.

Station tracks provide good access to most parts of
the area. Cross country travel in four-wheel drive vehicles is
no problem in the sandy plain and on the Pandurra ridges, but
becomes almost impossible in the sand dune country.
