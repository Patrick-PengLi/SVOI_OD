INTRODUCTION

The following report summarizes investigations carried out in Special
Mining Lease No, 139A during the six month period 1 June 1970 to 30
November 1970. It presents additional information to that presented in
the previous report entitled
Report of Exploration and Investigation
in S.M.L. No. 139A

The principle activity this year has been an investigation into the extent
and continuity of mineralisation in the East and West Lagoon areas. In the
case of the former there are still some areas awaiting further investigation,
but these have been temporarily left until the lagoon surface is drier.

The drilling in the West Lagoon is still continuing, the first two stages
of a three stage programme have been completed. .

Further exploration in the lagoon areas has commenced with the drilling
of an electrical (I. P. ) anomaly ( as mentioned in the May 31. 1970 report)
and the clearing of drill access trenches across the Gunyah Lake to permit
stratigraphic drilling.

Outside the Gunyah Lake the only drilling has been a traverse between
the Main Open Cut and some Biogeochemical drilling near the treatment plant.
This drilling was undertaken to test the theory of a syncline in the Pandurra

Formation, with the Main Open Cut and the House workings on one flank.

Preliminary reconnaissance has been undertakén in other areas of the
Lease, primarily around the Ironstone Lagoon area and near the old

Manganese workings on the western shore of Pernatty Lagoon.

The mineralogy of the ore has been much as indicated in the
C.S.I.A.O. report by McAndrew, with a preponderance of "Chalcocite", and
subsidiary Bornite, Chalcopyrite and a slight trace of native copper. In
some areas appreciable quantities of pyrite have been observed. Apparently
the heaviest concentrations of pyrite are in the Whyalla Sandstone above
the copper minerals, but this hasn't been investigated systematically.
It is intended to pursue more detailed investigations of the pyrite
distribution in the West Lagoon area.

In the Western part of this area there is a strong development of
of a hanging wall lens of copper, just below the contact of the Tregolana
Shale and the Whyall Sandstone. This lens is about 2 to 3 feet thick,
varying in grade about 1-26, It is hoped to have studies made on this to
compare the mineralogy of the lens with the main ore horizon.

During the second drilling programme a series of samples of
Tregolana Shale were taken at a depth of 5 feet below the topographic top
of the rock, so that this unit could be investigated for rock geochemistry,
these being samples over a known mineralised area for comparison with other

areas. No results are yet available from this programme.

2. DRILLING IN THE WEST LAGOON AREA.

One drilling programmme started in this area on 3 September
1970, and is still in progress, Drill hole numbers commenced with 3-2-1P.
No summary of drilling is yet available as all the information is being
coded and will be summarised as part of a computer programme in the near

future.

Drilling in this area has been broken into three stages, The
first stage was on 30m. N+S x 25m. E-W spacing, designed to straddle the
ore limits and to broadly indicate the depths of mineralisation, the ma
positioning of holes being largely based on information from earlier
drilling, Closing in the N-S spacing to 15m. was the second stage,
using target depths shown by the first stage. This stage was completed
a few days ago, Shortly the third stage will commence, which entails the
drilling of fill in lines within the areas considered to contain ore, so
that in the ore zones the final spacing will be 15m N-S and 12.5m,. E-W.

The high density of drilling in this area has been considered
necessary because of the obviously erratic nature of the ore in the East
Lagoon area, and still further information will be obtained during mining

by the sampling of selected blast holes.

Because of the imminent change over to metric values and the
correction of grid values to coincide with national ‘grid figures, no final
drawings are yet available for this area, In the next report there should be

a series of both plans and sections to accompany it,

During drilling a visual estimate of pyrite content has been
made, so that it will be possible to get some idea of the pyrite distribution
relative to copper. In addition, for selected holes, assays are being obtained
for lead, zinc and bismuth, as well as for copper and silver, as part of an
investigation into the orebody, All this information will be coded so that it

will be possible to use computer services to assist calculations.
