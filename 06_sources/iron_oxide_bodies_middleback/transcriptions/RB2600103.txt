          MAGNETIC SURVEYS IN THE MIDDLEBACK RANGES.


I.   INTRODUCTION.                                         . -
         Forsbout six weeks towards the end of 19U8, geophysical
observations were undertaken together with geological work in the
Middleback Ranges, South Australia, for the purpose of ascer-
taining to what extent geophysical work could be used for the
location of further deposits if oore, iron and to assist
in the elucidation of the general geological structyre of the
area.   It had "been known for some that the area was magnetically
disturbed, and consequently magnetic observations only were
carried but, in the hope of being able to.elucidate the mag-
netic anomalies.     The area does not, on cursort examination
seem particluarly suitable for other types of geophysical
work,   ^he observations were undertaken dm part also to in-
vestigate the suitability of the area for an airborne
magnetometer survey.     Some work of this nature has since been
carried out.
II AREA INVESTIGATED

                Firstly the Iron Prince Hill was selected as
the most suitable area to find out the possible correlation
of magnetic observations with known bodies of iron ore; the
geology of this area has been w ell established and the Hill
is free from the magnetite-bearing haematite quartzite
formations which form such a prominent feature of the rest of
the Middleback Ranges.    The observations at the Iron Prince
were carried out as a series of traverses along the lease
boundaries of the area with subsequent observations along
offsets at right angles to these traverses.
                Secondly, two traverses were aliso observed.
along the southern boundary of the Iron Queen and South Iron
Baron leases in order to ascertain the variations of magnetic
intensity over large masses of haamatite - quartzite.
                  Thirdly in order to ascertain the regional magnetic
variation outside the ranges^ a long magnetic traverse, with
stations a quarter to half a mile apart was carried out along
the Kimba Road.
Ill   TECHNIQUE
              Observations were initially carried out with Watts
vertical magnetic force variometer No. 1+6898. This instrument
was made available to the Department by Enterprise Exploration
Co. Pty. ^Hid, of Broken Hill     The instrument had been adjusted
to a sensitivity if approximately 11+ gammas per scale division
by W.G. Fenner of this Department for his observation in the
Wallaroo-Moonta area.      This high sensitivity was unnecessary
for the observations and some time could have been saved
by using a lower sensitivity as the instrument is somewhat
sluggish at high sensitivitym but having commenced observations
with this sensitivity, it was decided not to alter it;
the average distance apart of stations in the area was 200
feet over rough ground,•and very little actual economy in
time would have been adieved.      On 27th November 19U8 the
Department took delivery of a new vertical magnetic force
variometer No. 60565* and observations were commenced with this
instrument which had a sensitivity of nearly 30 gammas per scale
division.
                  For the purpose of obtaining   accurate Drift curves
most of the stations in the area were repeated.      The magnitude
of the magnetic anomalies obtained subsequently proved that this
refinement was unnecessary.
                  'The sensitivity of both instruments was ckeched
every two or three days by standardization with their auxiliary
magnets, and by interchanging these magnets in different instru-
ments, the magnets were checked against each other.       During the
period of the observations, that is from 22nd October,
until 9th Depeember, the sensitivity of instrument No.
1+6898 changed in sensitivity from 1U.1 to 11+.3 gammas per scale
division; and instrument No. 60565 from 29.0 to 29•3 from
29th November until 9th December;   over the same periods the
constants of the auxiliary magnets changed,with respect to
one another by less than fealf of one per cent, and the
constants of the magnets of instrument 60565, calibrations
of which had been supplied by Greenwich Observatory, agreed
in ratio to the same accuracy.
                    The average drift in a morning or
afternoon between repeated stations was approximately 10
gammas, but after correction for drift the error on normal
readings should be less than 10 gammas except for very large
readings. Aa    he anomalies even on the Iron Prince Hill
 are of the order of several hundred gammas, it is evident
that no appreciable instrumental erros have been introduced.
          The errors arising from superficial magnetic effects
on the ground are more difficult to estimate, and are qu$te
large in the most anomalous areas, up to ficty or a hundred
gammas, but tests made in areas of si ight variation indicate
that they are in general less than ten to fifteen gammas.     The
effect on the results from superficial magnetic effects is thus
also negligible.
IV RESULTS -
          The vertical Magnetic Intensity over the Iron Prince
Hill is shown ih Fig 1, which also shows the known outcrops
of haematitie ore . (A geological pain of the same area,
obtained through the courtesy of the Broken Hill Proprietary
Co. Ltd. is shown in Fig. 1 A blue areas represent haematite
ore).   It is seen that there is concentration of the
magnetic contours over the ore, with magnetic highs on the
horth and east side, and magnetic lows on the south, with
a maximum variation of just over 1000 gammas.   The included
areas of amphibolite produce further complexity of the
contours as they break the magnetisation of the main
ore body thus forming an extra magnetic high and low at the
boundaries of the amphibolite. The ore body is delineated
to approximately the accuracy to be expected from stations
200 ft. apart, that is to about   + feet.   It should be possible
to delineate the limits of the ore considerably more accurately
by more closely spaced stations, possibly to within + 20 feet.
Haematite itself is almost non-magnetic and limonite only
slightly magnetic, so the magnetic effects from the
haematitic ore bodies are presumed to arise from the presence
of small particles of magnetite included in the ore.

         In order to observe how the vertical magnetic
intensity was affected by depth inside the body, Magnetic
profiles are observed across the top and inside tunnel 1A;
these are shown in Fig(ifc) together with the geological
section of the hill along the line,   Little extra information
can be obtained from these results, and since they can only
be obserbed in lpcalities where underground workings exist their
application is very limited, it can perhaps be inferred that
the maximum depth of ore should be between U00 and 700 feet
from the.eastern end of the tunnel.
        .The two east-west profiles along the south end of the
Iron Queen and South Iron Baron leases are shown in Fig (3)»
together with annotations on the surface geology along
the southern traverse.   Phenomally large variations in
magnetic intensity were obtained over the uptilted haematite
quartzite strata, the highest peaks being over 50,000 gammas
in magnitude.   It is evident that it would not be easy to pick
out in such areas small anomalies of the order of 1,000 gammas
obtained from ore bodies.   The geology ©hows   fair general
correlation with the magnetic profile; it is considered that
much better correlation could be obtained from a regional
magnetic survey expressed as a contoured plan.
         Fig. (1+) shows the variation of magnetic intensity
along the 'Kimba Road which runs from Whyalla to Kimba through
the Kimba Gap in the Middleback Ranges.      The traverse extends
from twelve miles east of the Kimba Gap to four miles west.
One of the geological problems of the Middleback Ranges is
whether or not the haematite profile does not give sufficient
information to decide this point.      The relatively small
magnetic anomalies produced in the gap may be accounted for
either by the effect of large magnetic poles produced at
the South End of the iron Warrior on the north side of the
Gap, and at the north side of Cook's lease and the North Iron
Knight, on the South side of. the Gap, or by the effect of deep
seated hameatite quartzite, 100 feet or more below the
surface in the gap.      It is certain that the haematite
quartzite does not lie immediately below the surface in the Gap.
It is considered that by making further traverses through the
Gap, and across the ends ofthe range near the Gap, the
absence or otherwise of haematite quartzite under the gap
could be demonstrated. A cursory examination of the geology
along this traverse was made by Dr. K.R. Miles and reasonable
correlation occurs between geological features and magnetic
anomalies.   As the stations are a quarter to half a mile
apart, many minor features in the magnetic traverse may habe
been missed.      The most interesting feature of the traverse is the
anomaly at its extreme west end; this is in line with the
geometrical extension of the Camel Range, which contains
.similar haematite quartzite formations to the main Middleback
Range.   The magnetic profile indicates that these formations
must persist under the surface as far south as the Kimba Road.
The surface in this area consists- of limestone ridges, sand
dunes and wind blown deposits of seed gypsum.


V   CONCLUSIONS
       It is considered that a magnetic survey over proved
haematitic ore bodies can be used to delineate the approximate
limits of the ore and to indicate the presence of bodies of
amphibolite or other non-magnetic or slightly magnetic minerals
included in it.
     With present knowledge it is not considered possible to
use magnetic observation to locate ore bodies in areas containing
large masses of haematite-quartzite formations
      Magnetic methods, including also at times aerial magnetic
methods, can be used with advantage to interpret geological
structure in the Middleback Ranges by tracing haematite quartzite
still more so to find and trace similar buried structures outside
the area of the ranges, such as the extension of the   Camel Range
